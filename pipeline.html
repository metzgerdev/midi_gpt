What that one equation means the whole input, unpacked

Think of a session bassist. At any instant they are holding four things in mind: the note they just played, where they are in the bar, whether the kick drum lands on this beat, and what chord the band is on. They do not weigh these one at a time — the four fuse into a single sense of here is where I am, and the next note follows from that. The equation is that fusion, written down.

tok_emb The note it just played — A1 7.71
pos_emb Where it is in the four bars — step 5 of 64 7.71
grid_proj(grid) Whether a kick lands here — yes 5.56
chord_proj(chroma) Which chord is sounding — F major 4.08
= x Everything it knows at this instant 11.97

Right column: the length of each vector, and of their sum. Not 25.06, because they point in different directions.

Adding four things together sounds like it should destroy them — mix four tins of paint and you cannot unmix them. It works here because there are 128 dimensions to spread across, and training pushed each signal into its own set of them. Measured, any two of the four are as unrelated as randomly chosen directions. So the sum stays legible in the same way four instruments in different registers stay audible in one mix: nothing is lost, because nothing is competing for the same space.

The lengths in that last column are a mix balance. The note and the position are loudest; the kick and the chord sit underneath at roughly half — present enough to steer the next choice, not so present that they drown out what was actually played. Nobody set those levels. They are what training settled on.

Where the training data came from mine_midi_bass.py

None of this runs at inference — it produced the checkpoints. Bass and arp were mined from the MIDI corpus of a sibling project, multitrack-collaborator/, whose songs come from sample packs (JAFUNK, SOUKG, OSG) rather than from a model.

Per file MIDI → tokens
  1. merge tracks, pair note-on/off
  2. quantize to a 16th grid — ticks_per_beat / 4
  3. mono-reduce — lowest for bass (reese → fundamental), highest for arp
  4. octave-fit — median to E2 (bass) / C4 (arp)
  5. split into 64-step chunks
  6. drop chunks under 3 notes

Labels, self-supervised Nothing was annotated by hand
  1. grid = the stem's own note-ons
  2. per bar: pitch-class histogram
  3. score against every chord template — on-tone mass − ½ off-tone
  4. best match becomes that bar's chroma
  5. 12 transpositions rotate notes and chroma together
  6. — which is what forces the model to read the chord track
grid [64]  +  chord [64, 12]

Key is deliberately not normalized. Rotating notes and chroma together across all twelve keys leaves the chroma→notes mapping as the only invariant, so absolute pitch is useless to memorize.

The augmentation happens twice

genre_corpus was already expanded to 12 keys by that project's own augment_transpose.py before the miner ever saw it, and content-MD5 cannot detect it — a transposed file is different bytes. Behind the 354 "unique" sources sit roughly 151 distinct songs, so the same material can appear up to 144 times at different pitches. Mining data/genre_corpus instead of genre_corpus_aug would let the 12-key pass happen once.

Grid and tokens only agree 70.6%

The grid records raw note-ons; the tokens record pitch changes after mono-reduction. A note starting underneath a lower sounding note marks the grid but cannot appear in the token stream. Across 300 sampled examples they match exactly in 90 of 300. At inference the grid is a kick pattern anyway — a different signal — but it puts a ceiling on how tightly kick-lock can ever score.

The model is more staccato than its corpus

Training weighted pitch tokens ×3 and sustain ×0.3 to cure a sparse-drone failure, but left rest at ×1. Relative to sustain the model therefore sees rest as 3.3× more attractive than the corpus says, and restrikes where the source material holds.

Which weights run resolved by --base / --<role>-ckpt

Default is the highest fine-tuned checkpoint, so generation is on-policy: new hand-edits target residual taste rather than re-teaching what earlier rounds already fixed.

Deliberately not here

Every path from note to audio was removed. The drum loop stays because it is an input — it is analyzed for its kick pocket and never mixed into anything, which is why librosa and scipy are still required dependencies.
